# SUIT-DYN — Technical design

**Learning and forecasting solar atmospheric dynamics from Aditya-L1/SUIT**

Status: Phase 0 (literature and data audit). Nothing in this document is a result except §0, which is
a measurement of the data on disk, regenerated by `scripts/phase0_audit.py`.

> **Superseded in part by [PHASE1.md](PHASE1.md) (2026-09-27, 2.2 days of data).**
> - **Registration** now uses image motion with the fixed detector pattern removed, anchored to a
>   circle limb fit. It no longer uses a limb fit alone: the harmonic limb model was 3–4× noisier.
> - **Pointing** is jitter (~1.4 px per frame) plus a ~1.5–2 h oscillation (±10 px) plus jumps
>   (5–9 px), not a slow drift.
> - **New artefacts:** a fixed detector pattern (~4 % of the disk, network-like), a seam that varies
>   by row (up to −28 %), too-bright first frames of program blocks, and an instrument-state change
>   on 24 Sep.
> - **Pipeline B:** each multi-filter snapshot is a ~5-min burst, kept asynchronous.
>
> Where the two documents disagree, PHASE1.md holds.

Every design choice below depends on this section, so it comes first.
Source: `D:\Data\pradan1.issdc.gov.in\al1\protected\downloadData\suit\level1`. All figures come from
`outputs/phase0/audit.json`. The download was still running during the audit, so the counts will grow.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Files / volume | 6,745 Level-1 FITS, 12.1 GB (one `.part` file still downloading) |
| Time span | **One day**: 2026-09-25 01:00 → 23:30 UT |
| Processing | All `Lev1.0`, `V1`; one flat field (`master_flat_2024-05-24`); `QVAL = 100`, `QDESC = "Complete Image"` on every file |
| Flare flags | `SOLX1TR`, `SOLX2TR`, `HELIOSTR`, `NORM_FLR`, `PROM_FLR` = 0 on every file (no flare mode on this day) |

**Three kinds of frame, with very different value for this project:**

| Frame | Size, plate scale | Filters | Cadence | Share | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full disk, binned | 2048², 1.396″/px | **NB03 (Mg II k) only** | **~87 s** (p90 89 s) | 692 | **The only dense time series** |
| Full disk, unbinned | 4096², 0.698″/px | all 11 | ~2.3 h (one full filter cycle) | 72 | Multi-filter context; far too sparse for dynamics |
| ROI | 560², 0.698″/px | 9 (all except BB01, BB03) | ~88 s per filter | 5,981 | Two **operator-chosen** targets, tracked |

**Contiguous NB03 runs** (a gap over 5 min ends a run): **9.5 h (404 frames)** and **6.4 h (262 frames)**.
The rest are fragments of under 21 min. Between the runs there are gaps of 1 h and 3 h, and the 3 h gap
coincides with a change of observation ID and program (`OBS_MODE` 139 → 151).

**Artifacts found in the first look** (these become design requirements in §5):

1. **The Sun is off-centre on the CCD and partly off it.** Disk centre is at about (1280, 600) of 2048
   with R ≈ 697 px, so the southern limb is cut off and **3–4 % of the disk is not on the detector**.
   This is the SUIT–VELC misalignment described by Sarkar et al. 2025.
2. **Pointing drifts** by 17 px in x and **33 px (46″) in y** within the day.
3. **The off-limb sky is negative**, with a median of −400 to −456 counts. The scatter correction
   over-subtracts, so off-limb intensities cannot be taken at face value.
4. **Spikes.** Frame maxima range from 16,800 to 62,000 counts while the disk p99.9 is about 9,200.
   Level 1 does no spike removal; that happens at Level 2, which is not in the archive.
5. **A seam at the quadrant boundary.** A vertical step at x ≈ 1024 (binned) shows that the four CCD
   quadrants have residual gain or bias mismatch.
6. **Ghost and scatter halo** in the unbinned NB08 frames. Ghost removal runs only when the Sun is at
   the CCD centre, and here it is not.
7. **A level step across the program change.** The median disk intensity is about 3,220 before the
   3 h gap and about 3,150 after it (−2 %). We cannot yet say whether that is the Sun or the instrument.
8. **Exposure depends on the program.** NB03 is 300 ms in full-disk frames and 2,000 ms in ROI frames.
9. **ROIs follow two targets chosen by the operators** (`ROI_ID` 10–14, then 15–18). `X1` steps with
   solar rotation. These are the regions a person decided were interesting, so the ROI data are a
   selected sample.
10. **The quality flag carries no information**: 100 on every frame. QC has to be built by us.
11. **PSF.** The Sun sits towards the CCD corner, where Sarkar et al. measure an 80 % encircled-energy
    radius about twice that at the centre. Resolution therefore varies across the disk.

**Storage.** A binned NB03 frame is 8.4 MB, so the NB03 series alone is about 8.3 GB/day, about
250 GB/month and about 3 TB/year. D: has 119 GB free. **The raw archive cannot be held locally
beyond about two weeks.** This single fact shapes §6.

**Compute.** One RTX 3050 laptop GPU with 6 GB. PyTorch 2.14 with CUDA is installed; SunPy is not.

### What this means for the proposal

- **"SUIT spatiotemporal dynamics" in practice means Mg II k full-disk dynamics at 87 s and 1.4″.**
  All other filters are either 2.3-hourly (full disk) or operator-selected (ROI). A multi-channel
  temporal model can only be trained on the ROI subset, which is biased.
- **An 80–120M-parameter model is not justified.** With one day of data, and even with a year, the
  data support a model of about 5–30M parameters trained on SUIT alone. The GPU also rules out the
  larger size.
- **Horizons cannot be chosen yet.** Today, the longest contiguous run caps a context of 1 h plus a
  horizon at about 8 h, and only inside one run. §8 gives the rule for choosing horizons from the real
  archive.
- **The data audit must be repeated on a multi-month archive before Phase 2.** One day cannot show
  secular trends, CCD degradation, eclipse seasons, safe-mode gaps, or how often programs change.

## 1. Research hypothesis

**H0 (null):** Once the known deterministic motion (differential rotation) and the instrument's
effects (pointing, exposure, quadrant gains, program changes) are removed, SUIT Mg II k full-disk
sequences contain no forecastable structure beyond what persistence and optical-flow advection
capture at any horizon ≥ 15 min.

**H1:** A representation learned without labels from SUIT sequences gives forecasts of future
**derotated** observations, or of their learned representation, that beat those baselines on
**held-out future Carrington rotations**. The same representation also carries information about
independently measured solar evolution (plage area change, AR emergence and decay, flare-related
brightenings) that simple image statistics do not.

The comparison has to be against *derotated persistence*, not plain persistence (see §10). Plain
persistence on a rotating Sun is a straw man: a model that only learns rotation beats it easily and
has learned nothing new about the solar atmosphere.

## 2. Research questions (sharpened)

| # | Question | Operational test |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | How far ahead is SUIT evolution forecastable? | Skill vs derotated persistence and optical flow, per horizon; the horizon where the 95 % CI of skill first includes 0 |
| Q2 | Does self-supervised pretraining help forecasting? | Same dynamics model on (a) SSL latents, (b) randomly initialised encoder trained end-to-end, (c) frozen generic features (DINOv2), (d) frozen solar FM features (SDOFMv2/Surya on AIA 1600) |
| Q3 | Does solar-aware masking help? | E5 vs E4, same compute budget, linear-probe and forecast metrics |
| Q4 | Does an AR-aware local branch help? | E6 global+local vs global-only vs local-only, scored separately on AR pixels and quiet-Sun pixels |
| Q5 | Is latent forecasting better than pixel forecasting? | Both decoded to pixels and scored with the same metrics; also latent forecast → probe targets |
| Q6 | Does latent forecast error flag unusual evolution? | Event association against *independent* catalogues, with instrument events as negative controls (§14) |
| Q7 | Does the representation transfer? | Frozen probes: plage fraction, Mg II k disk-integrated excess, NOAA AR count / McIntosh class, GOES flare occurrence (last, and only as a probe) |
| Q8 | Does SUIT add information over SDO? | Predict SUIT latents from co-temporal AIA 1600/1700 + HMI. Explained variance near 1 → SUIT is largely redundant for this purpose |

## 3. Scientific novelty (honest)

**Already done, so not novel on its own:**
- Masked autoencoders on solar images: SDOFM, SDOFMv2 (Hong et al. 2026), Surya, Solaris.
- Video transformers for flare forecasting from HMI (Riggi et al. 2025; Li et al. 2025). They add
  little over single images.
- YOLO feature detection on SUIT (Seth et al. 2025).
- Case studies of SUIT pre-flare chromospheric transients (Adithya H. N. et al. 2026, arXiv
  2607.26171).

**Potentially novel:**
1. The first self-supervised, forecast-oriented representation of **full-disk Mg II k at 87 s
   cadence**. SDO has no Mg II k imager, and IRIS has Mg II only over a small field of view.
2. A **rigorous forecastability study** of chromospheric UV evolution: horizon limits measured
   against strong physical baselines (derotation, advection) on sealed future rotations. A carefully
   supported negative result ("no skill beyond advection past X h") would still be publishable.
3. A **latent anomaly score validated against instrument negative controls**. Most solar ML anomaly
   papers do not test whether their anomalies are just instrument events.
4. **Q8: an information-overlap measurement between SUIT and SDO.** It is useful to the Aditya-L1
   community whatever the answer.

The architecture itself (encoder, latent dynamics, decoder) is **not** the contribution. Reviewers
will treat it as standard. The contribution is the data understanding, the baselines, and whether
the result is honest.

## 5. SUIT-specific data issues → requirements

| Issue (§0 #) | Risk if ignored | Handling | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Disk truncation (1) | The model learns a moving "edge of the world" as dynamics | Per-frame validity mask = on-CCD ∧ r ≤ 0.98 R⊙ ∧ not flagged; loss and metrics only on valid pixels | Unit test: masked-out pixels get zero gradient |
| Pointing drift (2) | Drift looks like evolution | Register every frame to a fixed heliographic grid using a fitted limb (not only the header CRPIX), check against the header | Residual limb-fit error < 0.3 px; header vs fit difference logged per frame |
| Negative off-limb (3) | Off-limb "dynamics" are calibration artefacts | Off-limb excluded in v1; revisit with Level 2 | — |
| Spikes (4) | Single-frame outliers dominate MSE and anomaly scores | Temporal median despike (a pixel > kσ above both neighbours in time and above its 3×3 median); fraction replaced logged | Injected-spike test; despike rate vs time |
| Quadrant seam (5) | The model learns a fixed seam and its interaction with rotation | Per-frame quadrant offset estimated on quiet Sun and corrected; the seam line and a 3-px margin masked | Seam step before/after; the model must not "forecast" the seam (probe) |
| Ghost / halo (6) | Artefact structures in unbinned frames | v1 uses binned NB03 only; unbinned used for context, with ghost-region mask | Visual QC gallery |
| Level steps at program change (7) | "Anomalies" at every program switch | Normalise each frame by its quiet-Sun median at the same μ (limb-darkening–corrected contrast); the original level is kept as metadata | Negative control: anomaly score vs program-switch times (§14) |
| Exposure differences (8) | Brightness depends on program | Divide by `MEAS_EXP`; never pass exposure or mode to the model as inputs | 300 vs 2000 ms NB03 comparison on overlapping ROIs |
| Operator-selected ROIs (9) | "AR branch" learns operator choices; leakage of expert judgement | Local branch crops **from full-disk frames** by our own AR detector; ROI data only in a separate, clearly labelled experiment | ROI-vs-crop comparison |
| Uninformative QC (10) | Bad frames pass | Our own QC: limb-fit residual, fraction of disk on CCD, despike rate, quadrant step, global contrast z-score vs a running window | QC report per day; rejected-frame gallery |
| PSF varies across the disk (11) | Resolution change with position looks like structure change | Record μ (disk position) per token; score metrics per μ bin; optional ablation with μ as input | Metric vs μ plot |
| CCD degradation / flat age | Slow trends look like solar-cycle change | Quiet-Sun contrast normalisation (above); a trend of the raw quiet-Sun level is tracked and reported | Monthly quiet-Sun level plot |

Principle: every correction is **reversible and versioned**. The reduced product stores what was
done and the parameters, and the original file name and checksum.

## 6. Data pipeline

```
PRADAN (archive of record)
  │ manual download (user)
  ▼
raw FITS (local, temporary; pilot window kept permanently)
  │ ingest: header → manifest row (all keys + checksum + file name)
  ▼
calibration/QC (per frame)
  exposure normalisation → spike removal → quadrant-offset correction
  → limb fit (centre, radius) → QC metrics → accept/reject + reason
  ▼
registration
  reproject to a fixed heliographic grid at a fixed plate scale
  (disk-centred, solar north up via CROTA2 + P angle, distance-normalised R⊙)
  ▼
normalisation
  μ-dependent quiet-Sun limb-darkening fit → contrast image I/I_QS(μ)
  ▼
masks: valid, on-CCD, off-limb, seam, spikes-replaced
  ▼
derivatives (separate, regenerable)
  • global: 512² disk image (≈2.8″/px)
  • local: AR crops at native 1.4″/px, tracked (§6.1)
  • derotated stacks: frames re-projected to a reference time for each window
  ▼
training store (Zarr) + manifest (Parquet)
```

**Keeping raw data, given the disk.** This is a decision for the user, with this recommendation:
keep raw FITS permanently for a **pilot window** (about 7 days). For the long span use
**ingest → reduce → verify → delete raw**, keeping the manifest with PRADAN file names and SHA-256.
Reproducibility then rests on "re-download these exact files from PRADAN + run pipeline version X".
The sister project already runs this way. The alternative is an external drive of about 4 TB/year
for raw NB03.

**Reduced-store budget.** 512² float16 global at 87 s ≈ 0.5 MB × 990/day ≈ 0.5 GB/day ≈ 180 GB/year.
That is still too much, so the store keeps **full cadence for the pilot** and **one frame per 3 min
for the long span**, about 60 GB/year, plus AR crops. The cadence sub-sampling is itself a variable
to test (does 87 s add anything over 3 min at the chosen horizons?).

### 6.1 Region extraction and tracking

The detector is our own and fixed, not learned: plage/AR candidates are thresholded contrast above
k·σ_QS, smoothed, with an area floor. Candidates are linked across time by predicted position under
differential rotation (Snodgrass rates) and cross-matched to **NOAA SRS AR numbers** when within
~5°. The AR identity is used for the region-generalisation split and for AR memory. It is **never**
a model input unless an ablation says so.

Risk: plage boundaries are threshold-dependent. We report sensitivity to k and do not tune it on
test data.

## 7. Dataset schema

**Manifest** (`manifest.parquet`, one row per raw frame, including rejected frames):
`frame_id, file_name, sha256, obsid, t_obs (UTC, ns), filter, frame_type, naxis, cdelt, crota2,
crpix_hdr, sun_centre_fit, r_sun_fit, dsun, p_angle, meas_exp, cmd_exp, obs_mode, roi_id, flare flags
(SOLX1TR…PROM_FLR, FLR_ED), flat/scatter/prnu cal files, f_level, f_ver, qc_* (limb residual, disk
on CCD, despike fraction, quadrant step, contrast z), accepted, reject_reason, pipeline_version,
calibration_version`.

**Store** (`suitdyn_v{N}.zarr`):
```
/global/image      float16 [T, 512, 512]   chunks (1, 512, 512)   contrast I/I_QS(μ)
/global/valid      bool    [T, 512, 512]   chunks (1, 512, 512)
/global/mu         float16 [512, 512]      (fixed grid → one array)
/global/time       int64   [T]             ns since epoch; index into manifest
/local/crops       float16 [N, 256, 256]   one crop per (AR track, frame)
/local/index       table: crop_id, track_id, noaa_ar, t, frame_id, lat, lon, area
/attrs             pipeline git commit, config hash, calibration version, grid definition
```

**Format choice:**

| Format | For | Against | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zarr v3 + Parquet manifest** | Chunked random access to any T window; partial reads; compression (Blosc/zstd); rich attrs; many concurrent readers | Many small files on NTFS (mitigate with sharding in v3) | **Chosen** |
| WebDataset | Fast sequential streaming, good for multi-node | Random temporal windows need pre-baked sequences; changing window length means re-sharding | Only if we move to multi-node |
| HDF5 (chunked) | One file, mature | Single-writer; file locking and corruption risk on Windows; awkward with multiprocess DataLoaders | Rejected |
| Pre-cut `.npy` sequences | Simplest | Duplicates data per window length; horizon changes need a rebuild | Rejected |

Training samples are **index lists** (start time, context length, horizon) drawn from contiguous
valid runs. They are not materialised copies.

## 8. Sequence construction and horizons

Nothing is invented; horizons are chosen by rule. Given a cadence c (~87 s, or 3 min after
sub-sampling), a context length k and a horizon Δ, a window is **valid** only if:
1. every frame in [t−k, t+Δ] is accepted by QC, with gaps ≤ 2c;
2. there is no program change (`OBS_MODE`/`obsid`) inside the window. Windows that straddle one form
   a separate stress-test set;
3. the target frame's valid-mask overlap with the context is ≥ 90 %.

**Horizon rule:** candidate Δ ∈ {c, 15 min, 1 h, 3 h, 6 h, 12 h, 24 h}. A horizon is kept only if the
**test split** has ≥ 500 valid windows at that Δ, drawn from ≥ 10 distinct days. On today's data only
Δ ≤ 1 h passes, and only within the two runs. The rule is applied once, before training, and written
to the experiment registry.

**Context length k:** chosen on validation from {15 min, 1 h, 3 h}. It is part of ablation A (does
history help at all?).

**Derotation:** for every window, all frames are reprojected to the time of the last context frame
using differential rotation. The model forecasts **evolution in a co-rotating frame**. Limb-crossing
pixels are masked. This is the most important design decision in the document (§10).

## 9. Model architecture (initial, deliberately small)

```
contrast frame (512², derotated) + valid mask + μ-map
   │ patch embed (16² → 1024 tokens; invalid-patch token id)
   ▼
spatial encoder (ViT-S/16, ~22M) or ConvNeXt-T-lite (~10M)       ← E2/E3 choice
   │ per-frame latent: token grid Z_s(t) [1024 × 384] + pooled z(t) [384]
   ▼
temporal model on cached per-frame latents (k frames)
   factorised: attention over time per spatial token, shared weights
   (baseline: GRU; alternative: small SSM/Mamba-style)
   ▼
Z(t+Δ) prediction (token grid) ──► decoder (light conv upsampler) ──► Î(t+Δ)
                                └──► probabilistic head (§15), anomaly (§14)
```

**Budget:** 10–30M parameters total. Training at 512² with ~1024 tokens × k frames is too heavy for
joint attention on 6 GB. The design therefore **caches per-frame encoder latents** once the encoder
is pretrained (Phase 8) and trains the dynamics model on the cache. That is cheap and allows many
seeds.

**Local branch** (E6 only): the same encoder applied to 256² AR crops at 1.4″/px, and the tracked-AR
token sequence fused with the global token grid by cross-attention at the AR's grid location.

**Solar-aware tokens** (ablation K): patch embedding + learned 2-D position vs + (μ, heliographic
lat/lon, validity fraction). Heliographic coordinates are only defined for r < ~0.95 R⊙; beyond
that they are masked.

## 10. Baselines — the most important section

The weakest baselines in the proposal (persistence, ConvLSTM) would make almost any model look good.
Required, in order of strength:

| ID | Baseline | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| B0 | Persistence (raw frame) | Reference only; a straw man on a rotating Sun |
| **B1** | **Derotated persistence**: I(t) differentially rotated to t+Δ | Removes the largest, fully known signal; **the primary reference** |
| **B2** | **Optical-flow advection** (e.g. TV-L1 / DeepFlow on the last 2–4 derotated frames, extrapolated) | Lesson from precipitation nowcasting: advection is very hard to beat at short horizons (pySTEPS literature) |
| B3 | Linear temporal extrapolation per pixel (derotated), damped | Captures steady brightening/decay |
| B4 | Climatological mean contrast per μ + persistence blend (skill-optimal weight on val) | Guards against "the model just smooths" |
| B5 | ConvLSTM / PredRNN on derotated frames | Standard learned spatiotemporal baseline |
| B6 | ViT + temporal transformer, no SSL | Isolates the effect of pretraining |
| B7 | VideoMAE-style tube-masked encoder + same dynamics | Strong generic SSL |
| B8 | Frozen external encoders (DINOv2; SDOFMv2/Surya AIA-1600 encoder) + same dynamics | Is SUIT-specific pretraining worth anything? |

**Skill score** for every metric M: SS = 1 − M_model / M_B1 (and vs B2), reported with a 95 %
block-bootstrap CI over days.

## 11. Self-supervised objectives

Introduced one at a time; each must beat the previous on validation or it is dropped.

| Stage | Objective | Question |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Masked patch reconstruction (MAE, 75 % masking, loss on valid & masked only) | Does a spatial representation help at all? |
| S2 | S1 + temporal interpolation (mask frame t among t−2…t+2) | Does temporal context in pretraining help? |
| S3 | S1 + future-latent prediction (JEPA-style: predict the EMA-target encoder's Z(t+Δ)) | Predicting in latent space rather than in pixels |
| S4 | Contrastive (time-adjacent positives) | **Probably skipped**: near-identical frames make trivial positives, and rotation makes a shortcut. Only if S1–S3 plateau |

Interpolation (S2) has a known shortcut: the average of the neighbours is already a good answer.
The loss is therefore reported relative to the linear-interpolation baseline, not in absolute terms.

## 12. Forecasting objectives

Primary: P(Z(t+Δ) | Z(t−k:t)) on derotated sequences. Reported in two spaces:
1. **Pixel space** (decoded): comparable to B0–B5, and the only space in which a reviewer trusts
   "skill".
2. **Probe space**: forecast region-level quantities (plage area, integrated excess contrast per AR,
   disk-integrated Mg II k excess) and compare them with the same quantities measured from the
   observed future frame. This is where "useful" is judged.

Latent-space error alone is **never** reported as skill, because latent distances are not comparable
across models.

## 13. Loss functions

Start: masked L1 on valid pixels (L1 is more robust than MSE to leftover spikes). Then, one term at a
time, each justified:

| Term | Added when | Physical meaning |
|---|---|---|
| L_rec (masked L1) | always | Intensity (contrast) agreement |
| L_grad (masked L1 on ∇I) | if decoded forecasts are blurred (low gradient-preservation score) | Preserves edges of the network and plage |
| L_latent (MSE/cosine vs stop-grad EMA target) | S3 | — (representation consistency) |
| L_NLL (§15) | Phase 10 | Calibrated uncertainty |
| L_physics | **Not planned.** Candidate: a penalty on disk-integrated contrast change beyond the observed distribution. That is a statistical prior, not physics, and would be labelled as such | — |

There is no defensible physics loss for chromospheric UV intensity at this stage: we have no
magnetic field, no velocity, and no radiative-transfer model in the loop.

## 14. Anomaly detection

Score: A(t) = d(Ẑ(t+Δ), Z(t+Δ)), with d ∈ {L2, cosine, Mahalanobis under the predictive
distribution, NLL}. Scores are **computed per spatial token** and aggregated per AR and per disk,
so that an anomaly has a location.

**Validation (pre-registered before looking at scores on test):**
- **Positive events (independent):** GOES flares ≥ C1 on disk (HEK location), NOAA AR new-region
  appearance, SoLEXS/HEL1OS catalogue flares (sister project), Adithya 2026 pre-flare transients.
- **Negative controls (instrument):** program/obsid changes, exposure changes, pointing jumps >
  3 px, QC-borderline frames, CCD temperature excursions (housekeeping headers), quadrant-step spikes.
- **Test:** the event-association AUC of A(t) for positives vs matched quiet times, compared with the
  AUC for negative controls. **If the negative-control association is as strong as the solar one,
  the anomaly detector is an instrument monitor, and we report exactly that.**
- **Location:** for flares with HEK positions, check that the top-scoring token is within 100″ of
  the flare, vs a random-location null.
- **Lead time:** only after the above passes; with block bootstrap; flare labels are never used in
  training.

## 15. Uncertainty

Only after a deterministic model beats B1 and B2 (the gate in §23).
- **Candidates:** a heteroscedastic Gaussian head per token (cheap); deep ensemble of 5 (reference);
  quantile head on probe quantities. MC dropout is **not** used as the main method (it is poorly
  calibrated for distribution shift) but may serve as a cheap comparison.
- **Metrics:** CRPS (ensemble or closed-form Gaussian), NLL, PIT histograms, 50/90 % interval coverage,
  sharpness (mean interval width). Calibration is checked separately **for quiet Sun, AR and
  near-limb** pixels.

## 16. Evaluation metrics

All metrics are on valid pixels of the derotated frame, per horizon, as a skill vs B1 and B2 with a
day-block bootstrap CI.

| Level | Metrics |
|---|---|
| Image | MAE, RMSE, PSNR (on contrast), SSIM (masked) |
| Structure | Gradient-magnitude correlation, edge F1 (Canny on contrast), plage-mask IoU, per-AR area and integrated excess error |
| Temporal | Consistency: error growth vs Δ; trajectory error of AR centroid/area along tracks |
| Latent | Forecast error normalised by latent variance; representation stability (same frame, different augment/despike seeds) |
| Probabilistic | CRPS, NLL, coverage, sharpness, PIT |
| Generalisation | Future rotations; unseen NOAA ARs; stratified by μ and activity level |
| Downstream | Linear probes (plage fraction, AR count, McIntosh class, flare-in-next-24 h as the last probe); anomaly AUCs with controls |

Stratification by μ is mandatory because the PSF and projection change with disk position (§0 #11).

## 17. Ablation plan (each answers one question, same budget and seeds)

| | Ablation | Question |
|---|---|---|
| A | Context k = 1 frame vs k > 1 | Does history add anything beyond the current state? |
| B | No SSL (random init) vs SSL | Q2 |
| C/D | Random vs solar-aware masking (masking weighted towards AR/limb, never on invalid) | Q3 |
| E/F/G | Global / local / both | Q4 |
| H/I | Pixel-space dynamics (ConvLSTM on pixels) vs latent dynamics + decoder | Q5 |
| J | Deterministic vs probabilistic head | Does the probabilistic head cost point skill? |
| K | ± solar positional tokens (μ, lat/lon) | Is geometry information useful or a shortcut? |
| **L (added)** | **With vs without derotation** | How much apparent "skill" is just rotation? |
| **M (added)** | **87 s vs 3 min cadence** | Is high cadence informative at these horizons? |
| **N (added)** | Train on program 139 only, test on 151 | Program/instrument-state generalisation |

Each ablation uses ≥ 3 seeds; differences are reported with CIs. An effect smaller than seed-to-seed
spread is reported as "no effect".

## 18. Leakage prevention

- **Split unit: Carrington rotation.** Train on early rotations, validate on the next, test on later
  ones. Each boundary has a **2-day embargo** (longer than any context + horizon) where no windows
  are drawn.
- **AR generalisation test:** a test subset restricted to NOAA ARs that never appear in training.
  Recurrent ARs keep a new NOAA number on the next rotation but are physically the same region. We
  detect recurrence by Carrington longitude/latitude matching and exclude it from the "unseen"
  subset.
- **Normalisation statistics** (quiet-Sun fits, scalers) are fitted on train only; every such
  statistic is saved with its split.
- **The encoder is pretrained on train rotations only.** Pretraining on all data (even without
  labels) leaks the test distribution; it is a tempting and common shortcut.
- **Automated leakage tests** (in `tests/`): no frame ID in two splits; no window crosses an embargo;
  scaler fit uses only train IDs; perturbing test data changes no train artefact (hash check).
- **Test seal:** the test manifest is hashed and written at split time. Test evaluation needs
  `--unseal` with a pre-registered experiment ID and runs once per experiment; each call is logged.

## 27. What would invalidate the hypothesis (decision criteria, to be frozen in pre-registration)

- **H1 fails** if, at every horizon passing the §8 rule, the best learned model's skill vs **B2** has a
  95 % CI that includes 0 on the test rotations.
- **"SSL helps" fails** if the SSL encoder's forecast skill and probe scores are within seed spread of
  random-init end-to-end training at equal compute.
- **"Solar-aware masking helps" fails** if C vs D is within seed spread.
- **"AR branch helps" fails** if E6 does not improve AR-pixel metrics over global-only.
- **The anomaly claim fails** if the AUC for solar events is not greater than the AUC for instrument
  negative controls (CI-separated), or if location matching is no better than the random-location
  null.
- **"SUIT adds information over SDO" fails** if co-temporal AIA+HMI predict the SUIT latent with
  R² ≥ 0.9 on test.

## 28. Challenging the architecture (reviewer's view)

For each major decision: why, the alternative, the downside, and how it is tested.

**1. Latent-state forecasting rather than pixel forecasting**
- *Why:* pixel MSE rewards blur, and forecasting in a compressed space may make structure easier to
  predict.
- *Alternative:* forecast pixels directly (ConvLSTM, PredRNN, or U-Net with advection priors), or
  forecast only probe quantities.
- *Downside:* latent errors cannot be compared across models. The latent space may drop exactly the
  small-scale changes that matter (brightenings). An encoder trained with an MAE objective is not
  necessarily forecast-friendly. JEPA-style objectives can collapse.
- *Test:* ablation H/I, scored in pixel **and** probe space; collapse monitored by latent variance
  and rank.
- **My view:** the proposal treats Z(t) as the central object. A reviewer will ask why, when pixels
  are observable and interpretable. Keep latent forecasting as a hypothesis and make **pixel-space
  skill vs B1/B2 the headline metric**.

**2. Global + local branches**
- *Why:* 512² discards the 1.4″ detail in ARs.
- *Alternative:* one model at higher resolution on derotated tiles, or local-only (ARs are where
  change happens).
- *Downside:* two branches double the tuning space. The fusion design is arbitrary. Tracking errors
  add noise.
- *Test:* E/F/G, scored separately on AR and quiet-Sun pixels.
- **My view:** a local-only model on AR tiles may be the strongest *and* simplest design. Run F
  before building fusion.

**3. Self-supervised pretraining before forecasting**
- *Why:* there are no labels, and SSL helps when downstream data are scarce.
- *Alternative:* train the forecaster end-to-end on the forecasting loss. Forecasting is itself
  self-supervised: the future frame is the label. Or use frozen SDO foundation-model encoders.
- *Downside:* this is the most compute-heavy step, and the data are small. MAE pretraining on a few
  hundred thousand highly correlated frames may add little over end-to-end training.
- *Test:* ablation B and B8.
- **Key point:** the proposal frames "SSL representation" as the novelty. But *future prediction
  already is self-supervision*. The question is whether a *separate* pretraining objective helps.

**4. A transformer encoder (ViT/Swin/VideoMAE)**
- *Why:* global context and a standard SSL recipe (MAE).
- *Alternative:* a ConvNeXt/U-Net. Convolutions have a translation prior that suits rotating
  textures and are data-efficient.
- *Downside:* ViTs are data-hungry. Riggi et al. 2025 found VideoMAE on HMI *worse* than single-image
  models.
- *Test:* E1 vs E2 at equal parameters and compute.

**5. Solar-aware masking and tokens**
- *Why:* uniform masking wastes capacity on quiet Sun and off-limb.
- *Alternative:* uniform masking with loss weighting by region.
- *Downside:* AR-weighted masking may make the encoder overfit to high-contrast plage. μ and lat/lon
  tokens may become shortcuts (for example "limb = dark").
- *Test:* C/D and K.

**6. Anomaly score = latent forecast error**
- *Why:* no labels are needed, and it follows from the forecaster.
- *Alternative:* simple statistical detectors, such as a running-window z-score on derotated
  contrast per pixel. This is the **anomaly baseline** and must be included.
- *Downside:* forecast error is high whenever *anything* is unpredictable: instrument events,
  near-limb pixels, cosmic rays. It is also high for events that are *large* but not *unusual*.
- *Test:* §14 negative controls, plus the z-score baseline.

**7. An 80–120M-parameter target**
- **Rejected for now.** Neither the data (§0) nor the GPU supports it. Scale up only if a
  learning-curve experiment (skill vs training rotations) is still rising at the largest data size.

**8. Using ROI data**
- *Why:* it gives multi-filter, high-resolution sequences.
- *Downside:* operators choose the targets, so it is a biased sample, and it sometimes follows flare
  triggers, so the data themselves carry flare labels.
- *Decision:* kept out of the main line. It is a separate, labelled multi-filter experiment later.

**9. The deepest question: is chromospheric UV evolution forecastable beyond advection at all?**
- Plage and network evolve over hours to days, driven by flux emergence and diffusion that are not
  visible in Mg II intensity until they happen. Flares are minutes-scale and mostly unforecastable
  from intensity alone (Riggi et al. 2025; Leka et al. 2019).
- **Prior expectation:** skill over B2 appears at intermediate horizons (1–6 h) for slow plage
  evolution and decay, and near zero for rare impulsive events.
- Phase 5 (baselines only) is designed to test this cheaply **before any deep model is built**.
